# Task 1 round 2: loss/tie action and market timing

This round parses every cached loss/tie replay for submissions 56238321 (no-R128) and 56234455 (v42_pkg). It follows the recorded own seat, aligns actions to `observation.day`, and aggregates action types, SELL products/quantities, late-game activity (days 27–29), and callback latency. Reproduction: `python task1_round2_20260915.py`; machine-readable output is [TASK1_ROUND2_20260915.json](TASK1_ROUND2_20260915.json).

The cache contains 56 competitive non-wins for no-R128 (38 losses, 18 ties) and 11 for v42_pkg (10 losses, 1 tie). Every replay is 720 steps. The no-R128 non-win set therefore has a much larger sample and should carry more weight for diagnosing recurring failure modes; v42_pkg's ten losses are useful as a contrast, not a stable estimate.

Across both submissions, losses and ties execute the same broad phase schedule: movement (NORTH/WEST/EAST/SOUTH), WATER, HARVEST, CARE, COLLECT_FERTILIZER, FEED and HIRE dominate; there is no evidence of an early termination or timeout. Aggregate counts scale with the number of games. SELL is the key market action, with WHEAT the largest volume, followed by CARROT/FERTILIZER and then animal products. This ordering is consistent in both non-win sets and is not an opponent-specific single-product failure.

The most useful timing result is that late-game liquidation is highly active in all non-wins. On days 27–29, no-R128 losses contain 5,692 SELL events (3.21 million units); no-R128 ties 2,710 events (1.52 million units). The v42_pkg losses contain 1,498 events (0.84 million units). WHEAT remains the most frequent late sale, then CARROT and FERTILIZER; MILK and WOOL are substantial but are interleaved rather than sold as one final batch. This pattern is consistent with the high-score tape finding that order and price timing matter: a generic “merge all same-product SELLs” rule would erase the observed interleaving.

Action-level comparison of the two submissions shows no unique crash action in losses. Their common top actions are WATER, directional movement, PASS, HARVEST, SELL, CARE, COLLECT_FERTILIZER, FEED and HIRE. The failure signal is therefore cumulative state/value drift rather than a missing terminal step. Loss margins are modest on average (−1,366 no-R128, −2,036 v42_pkg) but have tails of −6,463 and −6,887 respectively; ties have exactly equal rewards and no timeout indicators.

For each non-win replay, the JSON output retains every own-seat SELL event with step, day, product, quantity, visible price and market inventory before the action. This supports follow-up checks of price slope, market depletion and whether a sale follows a BUY/SELL barrier. The current round does not infer causality from reward alone; it identifies the late-game market queue and inventory transitions that should be replayed in a controlled local engine experiment.

The two concrete next experiments are: (1) compare the final 72 steps (days 27–29) against a paired V42 control while preserving all earlier actions, and (2) test narrow per-product ordering policies that preserve split/interleaved SELLs and only alter order when projected inventory and visible price make the swap legal. Do not globally merge SELLs based on these logs.
